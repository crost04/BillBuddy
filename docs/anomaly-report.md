# Anomaly detection – evaluation report

Generated by `python -m billbuddy.ml.anomaly.evaluate` on 2026-10-29 (sample data). The generator inserted duplicates and unusually high amounts (4–8 × the normal amount) and marked them.

## 1. Data understanding

- 940 expenses, of which 13 are duplicates and 12 are unusually high.
- 1 of the 12 unusually high amounts are still smaller than the largest normal expense of their category (e.g. a 4 × grocery bill in a category where big shopping trips happen). No method that only looks at the amount can find those.
- 1 unusually high amount(s) are in categories with fewer than 8 expenses (e.g. travel), which are not checked: too little data to know what is normal.
- Amounts are skewed: many small, few large expenses. On the log scale they are roughly symmetric.

## 2. Unusually high amounts – method comparison

Each category is checked separately (a 300 € grocery bill is unusual, a 300 € hotel is not); categories with fewer than 8 expenses are skipped.

| Method | Found (correct) | False alarms | Missed | Precision | Recall | F1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| z-score (mean and standard deviation) > 3 | 8 | 4 | 3 | 66.7 % | 72.7 % | 69.6 % |
| IQR rule on euros (Q3 + 1.5 · IQR) | 10 | 19 | 1 | 34.5 % | 90.9 % | 50.0 % |
| Isolation Forest (scikit-learn) on log amounts | 11 | 132 | 0 | 7.7 % | 100.0 % | 14.3 % |
| Robust z-score (median, MAD) on log amounts > 3.5 | 6 | 0 | 5 | 100.0 % | 54.5 % | 70.6 % |
| Robust z-score on log amounts > 3.0 | 9 | 1 | 2 | 90.0 % | 81.8 % | 85.7 % |
| IQR rule on log amounts (chosen) | 10 | 1 | 1 | 90.9 % | 90.9 % | 90.9 % |

- Mean and standard deviation are pulled up by the outliers themselves (masking), so they miss some and still raise false alarms.
- The IQR rule on euros raises many false alarms, because large amounts are normal in a skewed distribution.
- The Isolation Forest is a machine-learning method; with its default setting it flags far too much. It would need the share of outliers in advance – which nobody knows in real use – and for a single number (the amount) it brings no advantage over simple statistics.
- The robust z-score with the value recommended in the literature (3.5) raises no false alarm but misses half of the outliers; with 3.0 it is close to the IQR rule.
- **Chosen: the IQR rule on log amounts** – the most outliers found with the fewest false alarms. We had first picked the robust z-score; the evaluation showed it was not the best choice for this data.

## 3. Threshold of the chosen method

| Factor k in Q3 + k · IQR | Found (correct) | False alarms | Missed | Precision | Recall | F1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0 | 10 | 11 | 1 | 47.6 % | 90.9 % | 62.5 % |
| 1.5 (used) | 10 | 1 | 1 | 90.9 % | 90.9 % | 90.9 % |
| 2.0 | 6 | 0 | 5 | 100.0 % | 54.5 % | 70.6 % |
| 3.0 | 1 | 0 | 10 | 100.0 % | 9.1 % | 16.7 % |

1.5 is the usual value (Tukey). A smaller factor raises more false alarms – and every false alarm asks the user a needless question; a larger one misses outliers.

## 4. Duplicates – method comparison

Evaluated per pair. Left: the duplicates exactly as generated (identical copies). Right: more realistic – the second person writes the purchase differently ("REWE", "rewe Kartenzahlung") and maybe one day later.

Identical copies:

| Method | Found (correct) | False alarms | Missed | Precision | Recall | F1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Exactly the same description, amount and date | 13 | 0 | 0 | 100.0 % | 100.0 % | 100.0 % |
| Same amount within 1 day (description ignored) | 13 | 0 | 0 | 100.0 % | 100.0 % | 100.0 % |
| Same cleaned description and amount within 1 day (chosen) | 13 | 0 | 0 | 100.0 % | 100.0 % | 100.0 % |

Realistic duplicates:

| Method | Found (correct) | False alarms | Missed | Precision | Recall | F1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Exactly the same description, amount and date | 3 | 0 | 10 | 100.0 % | 23.1 % | 37.5 % |
| Same amount within 1 day (description ignored) | 13 | 0 | 0 | 100.0 % | 100.0 % | 100.0 % |
| Same cleaned description and amount within 1 day (chosen) | 13 | 0 | 0 | 100.0 % | 100.0 % | 100.0 % |

- An exact comparison misses duplicates as soon as the spelling or the date differs.
- Ignoring the description works just as well here – but only because the generated amounts have random cents, so two different purchases never cost exactly the same. Real amounts are often round (two 10 € cinema tickets on consecutive days), which would cause false alarms. That is why the chosen method also compares the cleaned description; whether this matters will show with real data.
