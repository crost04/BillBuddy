## 2. Model comparison (backtesting)

16 months × 5 days. MAE = mean absolute error in euros, MAPE = mean absolute error in percent of the real month total.

| Method | MAPE (total) | MAE day 5 | MAE day 10 | MAE day 15 | MAE day 20 | MAE day 25 | Sum of MAE over categories |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Extrapolation (spent / day × days) | 69.5 % | 5.171 € | 2.126 € | 1.271 € | 801 € | 330 € | 2.708 € |
| Mean of previous months | 15.0 % | 526 € | 526 € | 526 € | 526 € | 526 € | 1.077 € |
| Last month | 24.7 % | 779 € | 779 € | 779 € | 779 € | 779 € | 1.317 € |
| Linear regression | 12.9 % | 627 € | 489 € | 559 € | 414 € | 154 € | 917 € |
| Spent + mean rest | 10.1 % | 457 € | 436 € | 428 € | 379 € | 114 € | 677 € |
| Spent + median rest (chosen) | 9.6 % | 457 € | 432 € | 437 € | 331 € | 120 € | 606 € |

- Extrapolation is by far the worst at the start of the month (rent on the 1st).
- The mean of previous months ignores what already happened this month.
- The linear regression has too few examples (one per month) and learns noise.
- "Spent + rest" uses both: what is known about this month and the typical rest. The median is more robust than the mean against single unusual months (holidays, big purchases).

## 3. How reliable is the range?

The app shows a range of ± one standard deviation of the rest. Share of cases in which the real month total was inside the range:

- Total: 61.1 % (average width 527 €)
- Per category: 76.8 %

So the real result lands inside the range in roughly two out of three cases – which is what ± one standard deviation promises (about 68 % for normally distributed values).
